Interviewer: Can you describe the major development challenges affecting your community?

Chief:
The most serious issue in this community is the condition of the roads. During the rainy season,
vehicles struggle to enter the area, and farmers cannot transport their produce to nearby markets.
This affects household income and discourages traders from coming here. Another challenge is education.
Some school buildings are old and overcrowded, and there are not enough teachers in the area.
Healthcare is also a concern because the nearest health center is far away, forcing pregnant women
and elderly people to travel long distances for treatment.

Interviewer: What about employment opportunities for the youth?

Chief:
Youth unemployment is becoming a serious social issue. Many young people complete school but cannot
find jobs. Some travel to cities while others become involved in illegal mining activities because
they believe there are no alternatives. We have repeatedly appealed to the district assembly and
other stakeholders for vocational training centers and support for agriculture.

Interviewer: How do traditional leaders contribute to development?

Chief:
Chiefs and elders organize communal labor, resolve conflicts, and support development planning.
We regularly hold meetings with the assembly and advocate for roads, electricity, water systems,
schools, and healthcare facilities. We also encourage peace and unity within the community.
